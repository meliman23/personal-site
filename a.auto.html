What You Can Learn From This Test:

✅ Wheel Turns, Engine Rotates Normally – Your engine isn’t seized, and the issue is likely elsewhere (starter motor, starter clutch, or electrical).
🚨 Wheel Stops Suddenly / Won’t Move – This could indicate an internal engine issue (seized piston, jammed starter clutch, or transmission problem).
🔄 Grinding or Weird Noises – Possible transmission or starter gear issue

How It Works:
Lifting the Rear Wheel
– By getting the rear wheel off the ground (using a rear stand or center stand), you can freely rotate it without resistance from the ground.
Putting the Bike in 6th Gear – Why 6th Gear? Because it's the highest gear, which means the least amount of engine resistance when turning the wheel. Lower gears (1st, 2nd) have more torque multiplication, making it much harder to turn the engine manually. In 6th gear, the transmission ratios are closer, so turning the wheel translates to a gentler movement of the crankshaft.
Rotating the Rear Wheel by Hand – When you spin the rear wheel forward, it transfers motion through the chain, into the transmission, and finally to the crankshaft. If the engine is free, the wheel will turn with some resistance from compression, but it should still move.
